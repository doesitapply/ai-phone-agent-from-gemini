# SMIRK Competitive Offer Scan — 2026-09-03

## Scope and Method

This is a packaging comparison for inbound AI receptionist, missed-call recovery, and human-receptionist alternatives relevant to small local service businesses. It is not market-size research and does not estimate competitor revenue. Every price and feature below is taken from the competitor's public product or pricing page accessed on 2026-09-03. A price described as "starting at" or an unpublished price is recorded as such rather than converted into an estimate.

## Evidence-Based Market Observations

The market splits into two different purchases. The first is **coverage**: somebody or something will answer, capture a lead, and route urgency. Dialzara, Goodcall, Smith.ai, Ruby, and Slang all price this in one way or another. The second is **operations software**: a business phone, inbox, CRM, SMS, analytics, and automation bundle. Frontdesk AI and Podium are closer to this second category. The products can share infrastructure, but they should not share a confusing first offer.

Competitors also separate **software subscription** from **implemented outcome**. Smith.ai explicitly moves from self-directed setup to dedicated onboarding and then full-service configuration/ongoing optimization as the tier rises. Slang sells white-glove, verticalized operation. Dialzara withholds ongoing custom prompt work until a higher tier. That supports an explicit paid onboarding/implementation service for SMIRK even though delivery is browser-based: the charge is for business discovery, forwarding setup, knowledge-pack review, escalation routing, and a controlled proof call—not for installing desktop software.

The customer-facing unit must be understandable. Every direct AI competitor exposes either calls, minutes, unique customers, locations, or an explicit outcome. SMIRK's existing 500 calls / 1,000 minutes hard cap is technically defined but commercially weak as a first explanation because it leads with the cost-control mechanism rather than the buyer's job. Any future limit still has to be disclosed in checkout and policy documents before it takes effect.

## Implications for SMIRK—Not Yet a Pricing Decision

1. Do not market a generic "Starter" and "Pro" split. It obscures whether the buyer is buying missed-call capture, a full answering operation, or paid configuration.
2. Do not launch a broad "full dashboard suite" as if every listed function is production-proven. The suite can be a design target and future assisted offer; it is not yet an automatic entitlement.
3. Sell an explicit **Business Launch / Implementation** service if Cameron performs the setup. It can be remote or on-site; browser delivery does not make the configuration work free.
4. Keep the narrow missed-call recovery offer commercially independent from future phone-operations functionality. A successful first proof call remains the prerequisite for presenting it as an automated recovery service.

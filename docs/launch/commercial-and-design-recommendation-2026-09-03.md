# SMIRK Commercial and Design Recommendation — 2026-09-03

## Decision

SMIRK should not sell generic software tiers named **Starter** and **Pro**. The buyer does not wake up wanting a dashboard; they want missed callers handled, qualified, and returned before the lead cools. The product should therefore be sold as a narrow recovery outcome first, with a separately priced human implementation service. A broader phone-operations product may be quoted later, but must not become a self-service promise until its live proof and support model exist.

> A browser-delivered product can still have an implementation fee. The fee pays for accountable business discovery, call-forwarding configuration, knowledge review, escalation design, user setup, and a controlled proof call—not software installation on a customer computer.

## Recommended Offer Architecture

| Commercial name | Customer outcome | Recommended commercial position | Current readiness | Sales rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SMIRK Recovery** | After-hours or missed calls are answered, a caller need is captured, and the owner receives one usable callback action. | Retain the currently approved **$197/month** price and 500-call/1,000-minute hard cap until a separately approved replacement policy and Stripe binding exist. | The narrow product path is the only one eligible for a self-service checkout after live proof. | Lead with the outcome, not minutes. Disclose the cap in checkout and policy. Do not market recovered revenue until a real proof establishes the evidence. |
| **SMIRK Launch** | Cameron configures the recovery operation for the actual business. | **Recommended:** $500 remote launch, or $1,500 on-site/local launch. This is a recommendation, not an active checkout price. | Can be offered as an assisted service only after an operator can complete the work reliably. | Includes business discovery, forwarding choice, Business Knowledge Pack review, caller escalation rules, owner alert routing, access setup, and a proof call. No hidden “installation” claim. |
| **SMIRK Operations Desk** | A supervised phone-operations workspace: calls, callback queue, alerts, settings, and approved business context. | **Recommended:** quote-only while it is being proved; do not publish or enable an automated purchase lane. A future $397–$599/month range is commercially plausible but is not approved product pricing. | Not self-service ready. Its capabilities and support expectations exceed the validated Recovery loop. | Sell only against a written implementation scope. Do not call it a full CRM replacement or an autonomous employee. |

The names describe a job the buyer recognizes. **Recovery** is the initial product. **Launch** is paid human work that accelerates a successful deployment. **Operations Desk** is a future assisted upsell, not a disguised rebrand of unproven internal controls.

## Why This Position Is Defensible

Published competitor offers support charging separately for implementation rather than hiding setup inside a generic tier. Smith.ai includes dedicated onboarding at Pro and full-service configuration/optimization at Enterprise; Slang AI uses white-glove support in higher tiers; Dialzara reserves custom prompt engineering for its more expensive offering. [1] [4] [5] The human-receptionist benchmark is materially more expensive: Ruby lists $250 for 50 minutes and $720 for 200 minutes, whereas direct AI alternatives commonly expose a lower-cost coverage product with transparent usage. [2] [3] [6]

| Market signal | Implication for SMIRK |
| --- | --- |
| AI coverage products disclose a comprehensible unit such as minutes, calls, or unique customers. [1] [3] [4] | Keep the hard cap machine-enforced and contractually visible, but frame the offer around recovery first. |
| Broad inbox, CRM, messaging, and automation bundles create an enormous expectation surface. [3] [7] | Do not lead with an “everything suite” until its critical integrations and support operation are proven. |
| Higher-priced products earn their price through verticalized setup and accountable assistance. [1] [5] | A human Launch service is not a workaround. It is an appropriate premium offer for a trades business that wants someone to own configuration. |

## Package Changes Required Before Any Payment Change

No price, Stripe Product, Payment Link, policy document, or billing rule should be changed by copy editing. A package is a contract boundary. A future commercial change must be done as one reviewed release that updates the Stripe product/price and link, fulfillment allowlist, runtime limits, checkout copy, public policy documents, system-health readiness, and regression evidence together.

The former public `SMIRK24` workspace-promo path and the founders fulfillment lane are now disabled locally. Public pricing is filtered to the single approved paid plan; hidden sibling Payment Link configuration now blocks first-dollar readiness rather than merely producing a warning. This prevents a buyer from reaching a direct legacy Stripe link that the public UI happens not to show.

## Earned Hacker-Operations Design Direction

The visual target is **earned operations intelligence**. Dark mode is the primary product identity; it should look like a disciplined, expensive field-operations system, not a gaming terminal or generic B2B dashboard. A light alternative, if kept, should be warm stone and graphite—never bright white.

| Layer | Primary treatment | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dark foundation** | Near-black graphite, oxidized charcoal, off-white type, and one signal green reserved for verified live state or a consequential primary action. | Creates authority without visual noise. |
| **Signature motif** | A thin call-signal trace that becomes a completed callback receipt: caller → need → owner action. It must display real data only. | Gives SMIRK a recognizable visual idea tied to its actual job. |
| **Public pages** | Sparse, high-contrast editorial composition; large outcome statement; one operational trace; one explicit CTA. | Makes the product memorable without simulated “AI magic.” |
| **Owner desk** | Dense but calm queue design. Calls, Tasks, Alerts, and tenant-scoped CRM/business context are primary; provider and system settings remain operator-only. Status color appears only when sourced from a current health, task, or call event. | Makes action completion faster for a contractor or operator. |
| **Alternate surface** | Warm gray/stone background, dark graphite text, muted mineral borders, same signal green semantics. | Offers daylight readability without losing the product’s identity. |

The prohibited patterns are clear: neon grids, constantly glowing controls, faux terminal commands, decorative binary, status counters not backed by data, fake call transcripts, and generic pill-card SaaS wallpaper. A dark interface is not “hacker” because it is black; it is earned when the visual system reveals trustworthy system state and reduces the time to act.

## Current Proof Boundary

The local contract suite now passes for the checkout-to-fulfillment evidence model, canonical Starter isolation, policy binding, recipient safety, buyer activation email semantics, and manual-fallback handling. The data-integrity audit also removed reachable fixture records and converts storage failure into explicit `503 DURABLE_STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` responses rather than fabricated empty queues.

None of that proves the released service. Railway is still serving an older deployment, and the later deployment path has queued, removed, or failed before a current release reached active state. The following remain mandatory live checks after one clean active deployment: the metrics refresh, failed-transfer callback fallback, paid Stripe webhook-to-workspace activation, Business Knowledge Pack activation, authenticated endpoint behavior, and one controlled proof call. The public offer must not be expanded before those checks pass.
